Effects: +1 Influence · grants the epithet Kallinikos

This man has received the title Kallinikos (Winner of a Noble Victory), an honorific reserved for those whose triumphs are as glorious as they are decisive. Unlike common victories, his are marked by great celebrations, poetic recountings, and sculptors ready to immortalize the moment. Grateful cities laud his name, and even his enemies acknowledge his skill, though they might add that ‘noble victory’ often depends on who writes the history books.

This man’s divine handsomeness has left the Greeks in awe, earning him the title of 'Kalos' (the Beautiful One). His perfect features and godlike presence are enough to make sculptors weep, poets faint, and rivals envious. It is said that even Apollo himself might hesitate to compete with such beauty. Krypteia or Crypteia ("hidden, secret things") was a tradition involving young Spartans, part of the agoge regime of Spartan education. Its goal and nature are still a matter of discussion among historians.\nYoung Spartan men who had completed their training at the agoge with such success that they were marked out as potential future leaders would be given the opportunity to test their skills and prove themselves worthy of the Spartan military through participation in the krypteia.\nAccording to Cartledge, Krypteia members stalked the helot villages and surrounding countryside, spying on the servile population. Their mission was to root-out potential sedition. Troublesome helots could be summarily executed. Such brutal oppression of the helots permitted the Spartans to control the agrarian population and devote themselves to military practice. It may also have contributed to the Spartans' reputation for stealth since a boy who got caught was punished by whipping.\nOnly Spartans who had served in the Krypteia as young men could expect to achieve the highest ranks in Spartan society and army. It was felt that only those Spartans who showed the ability and willingness to kill for the state at a young age were worthy to join the leadership in later years.\nThis man has gone through that process at some time.
