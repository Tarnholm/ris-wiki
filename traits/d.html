Decurion (Roman Imperial Decurion)

Appears on family members — generals, governors, leaders · never on 21 of the cultures.

Effects: +10 Tax Collection, +1 Unrest, -1 Squalor

Drawn from the curiales class, which consists of the wealthy middle class citizens of a town society, Decurions are members of a city senate in the Roman Empire, and the most powerful political figures at the local level, being responsible for public contracts, religious rituals, entertainment, and ensuring order. Perhaps most importantly to the imperial government, they also supervise local tax collection. Early in the imperial period, aristocratic citizens actively sought the post as a mark of prestige, as they would gain seats in the front row of the theatre and be accepted into the class of honestiores (honourable men). Once elected, they are expected to pay large sums of their own money to perform public works; decurions typically compete with each other to furnish the community with temples, baths and other public facilities.
