Office of Pontifex Maximus

Appears on family members — generals, governors, leaders · pulls against Ex Pontifex Maximus.

Effects: +2 Influence, +2 Law

This man holds the office of Pontifex Maximvs, "Great Bridge-Builder". He is the chief religious officer of the state, and most senior of all Rome's priests. His primary duty is to maintain the pax deorvm, or "peace of the Gods". Historically, the position was an invention of the early Republic, assuming the duties the King once held as "Rex Sacorvm, or King of all things Sacred. In a typically Roman solution, the new rulers of the Senate simply created a position that was superior to the Rex Sacorvm. He was called Pontifex Maximvs, and he was elected like any other office. He supervised all the various members of the priestly colleges, pontifices, augurs, fetials, and other minor priests, and was also responsible for the Vestal Virgins. His home was at the Domus Publicvs, a state-owned mansion, which he shared with the Vestals. His official headquarters, though, was the small temple of the Regia within the Forvm Romanvm.

Ornamenta Triumphalia

Appears on family members — generals, governors, leaders · pulls against Doubtful Courage, Disgraced · never on 21 of the cultures.

Effects: +1 Influence

Once Augustus came to power, the right to a triumph ended for anyone other than the Emperor (due to the obvious reasons of creating a potential rival), and so a substitute was created - the general was awarded the regalia of the triumph (ornamenta triumphalia) but not the right to a triumphal procession. The honours include the right to wear triumphal dress in public: the corona triumphalis (a gold coronet fashioned in the shape of a laurel-wreath with dangling gold ribbons); an ivory baton; the tunica palmata (a tunic embroidered with palm-leaves); and the toga picta ("painted toga"), a toga which is dyed entirely purple with embroidered gold border, a robe believed to have been the official dress of the Roman kings.
