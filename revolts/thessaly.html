The Thessalian Revolt

Breaks away: Thessalian League from The Antigonid Kingdom

The four Thessalian cities of Larissa, Pharsalos, Pherai and Trikka can break away from the Antigonid Kingdom and form the Thessalian League. It can only happen when a new Antigonid faction leader takes over, and only while the Antigonid Kingdom holds all four cities. The chance is much higher from turn 76 (252 BC), or once the Antigonid Kingdom has shrunk to 14 settlements or fewer.

Any campaign in which the Antigonid Kingdom holds Larissa, Pharsalos, Pherai and Trikka, whether it is played by a human or by the AI.

What sets it off

  • Each time a new Antigonid faction leader takes over, from turn 76 (252 BC) onwards, or at any time once the Antigonid Kingdom holds 14 settlements or fewer, there is a 25% chance of the revolt, as long as the Antigonid Kingdom holds all four Thessalian cities.
  • Each time a new Antigonid faction leader takes over at any point in the campaign, and the chance above does not apply or fails, there is a further 5% chance of the revolt, again only if the Antigonid Kingdom holds all four Thessalian cities.

What happens

  • At the start of the next turn Larissa, Pharsalos, Pherai and Trikka all rebel against the Antigonid Kingdom.
  • All four cities pass to the Thessalian League, which did not exist until now.
  • Each city gets a strong garrison of Thessalian League troops, 3.5 times normal strength, drawn from Akontistai, Greek Slingers, Greek Archers, Thessalian Peltasts, Greek Hoplites, Thureophoroi, Prodromoi, Perrhaibian Cavalry, Xystophoroi and Thessalian Cavalry.
  • When Larissa revolts, Taras receives 10,000 denarii.
  • The revolt happens only once per campaign.

Playing as the rebels

Only a human playing the Antigonid Kingdom is offered the rebels, because the revolt needs all four cities in Antigonid hands. After the revolt, at the end of an Antigonid turn, a message titled 'The Thessalian League Declares its Independence from Macedon!' offers to let you play as the Thessalian League. If you accept, you switch to playing the Thessalian League and a government building is placed in Larissa, Pharsalos, Pherai and Trikka.

The in-game message: The Thessalian League Declares its Independence from Macedon!

Sensing their opportunity upon the death of the Macedonian king, cities across the rich plains of Thessaly have united and declared their independence. The Thessalians have long tolerated the rule of their northern neighbour, a lesser evil to the infighting that plagued them in the past. Yet, they have suffered much in the ceaseless wars of the successors. Pella must be reminded that the title of archon of the League is not inherited, but given!

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

The Thessalian League existed as early as the archaic period, and so did its ties to Macedon. Philip II was notably elected executive of the League around 352 BC. He quickly tightened his grip on Thessaly, imposing garrisons and changing regimes. Macedonian kings would control Thessaly for about 160 years, gradually reducing its autonomy. There were a few rebellions, the most notable of which occurred during the Lamian War between 323 and 321 BC, led by Pharsalos. Thessaly was significantly weakened after its defeat by Antipater and Krateros. Other important revolts occurred during the reign of Antigonus III Doson around 229-228 BC, which ended with perioikic Phthiotic Achaia under Aitolian rule. The Thessalian League, or at least parts of it, was finally liberated by Rome in 197 BC. It expanded and became an important faction in Greece for over a century (Graninger, ‘Macedonia and Thessaly’ (2007), pp. 307-323).
