The Revolt of Miletos

Breaks away: Miletus from The Ptolemaic Empire, or whoever holds Miletos at the time

The city of Miletos breaks away from whoever holds it, at the start of the campaign the Ptolemaic Empire, and becomes the independent faction of Miletus. It can happen early if the city is unhappy, and it always happens by turn 16 (267 BC) at the latest.

Every campaign, whoever holds Miletos, human or AI.

What sets it off

  • If Miletos rebels by itself through unrest at any time, it goes over to Miletus.
  • From turn 10 (268 BC), at the end of each of its owner's turns, there is a 25% chance of the revolt if Miletos is disillusioned or unhappier.
  • If none of the above has happened, Miletos revolts at the end of its owner's turn on turn 16 (267 BC), whatever its mood.

Playing as the rebels

If you, the human player, held Miletos when the revolt began, a message titled 'Miletos regains its autonomy!' appears at the end of a turn after the revolt and offers to let you play as Miletus. If you accept, you switch to playing Miletus and a government building is placed in Miletos. No offer is made if an AI faction held the city.

The in-game message: Miletos regains its autonomy!

Miletos, the richest and most beautiful Greek city on the Ionian Coast, coveted by kings even before the reign of the opulent Kroisos, has chosen to remain independent following the severance of its ties with the Ptolemies by ousting the local tyrant.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

The relationship between powerful cities like Miletos and Hellenistic kings was complex. The amount of autonomy they enjoyed varied over time. After 259 BC, when the tyrant Timarchos was ousted by Antiochos II (App. Syr. 11.65), Miletos minted its own coins and made several treaties with Cretan cities until around 246 BC, when Seleukos II entered the scene. This was one of many periods of increased independence (Dmitriev, ‘City government in Hellenistic and Roman Asia Minor’ (2005), pp. 65-67). Yet, Milesians were not otherwise idle. Inscriptions record treaties made throughout the Hellenistic period. Many concerned isopolitea with other cities, such as Mylasa (I.Milet 3 146). Others the settling of Cretan mercenaries and their families in Milesian territory. This could ultimately have amounted to 4,000 new inhabitants, and could have been done expressly to increase the city’s military strength (Chaniotis, ‘War in the Hellenistic World’ (2005), p. 105). Some treaties dealt with wars fought by the city. For example, a conflict with Magnesia required allies (Mylasa) and manpower (Cretans), and came to an end in 196 BC after years of fighting (Syll. 3 588).

Do you want to discontinue your campaign and play as the newly emerged Miletos faction?

Opens the way to: Milesian Reforms.

What happens

  • At the start of the next turn Miletos rebels against its owner.
  • The city passes to Miletus, which did not exist until now.
  • Miletos gets a strong garrison, 3.5 times normal strength, drawn from Akontistai, Greek Slingers, Greek Archers, Greek Peltasts, Milesian Horophylakes, Milesian Hoplites, Thureophoroi, Prodromoi and Xystophoroi.
  • Miletus receives 10,000 denarii.
  • The revolt happens only once per campaign.
