The Great Egyptian Revolt

Breaks away: Egyptians from the Ptolemaic Empire

The native Egyptians of Upper Egypt rise against the Ptolemaic Empire and form their own faction, the Egyptians. In a normal campaign it can only happen after the first Ptolemaic reform. If a human chooses to play the Egyptians, the revolt happens at the very start of the campaign instead.

It can happen in any campaign; when a human plays the Egyptians it always happens at the start, and otherwise it depends on the Ptolemaic Empire's reforms and chance.

What sets it off

  • Once the Ptolemaic Empire has passed its first reform, there is a 5% chance at the start of each Ptolemaic turn that the revolt breaks out, whether the Ptolemaic Empire is played by a human or the AI.
  • If a human starts a campaign as the Egyptians, the uprising is set off at turn 2 (270 BC) and takes place at the start of turn 4 (270 BC).

What happens

  • In a normal campaign, Syene-Elephantine, Thebes-Megale Diospolis, Mikra Apollonos Polis, Panos Polis and Megale Apollonos Polis rebel at the start of the next turn, whoever owns them, and pass to the Egyptians.
  • In a campaign where a human plays the Egyptians, Syene-Elephantine, Thebes-Megale Diospolis, Mikra Apollonos Polis, Panos Polis and Sais rise and pass to the Egyptians, and a government building is then added in each of them.
  • The towns that go over to the Egyptians receive strong garrisons of Egyptian Skirmishers, Egyptian Slingers, Machimoi Archers, Ptolemaic Machairophoroi, Egyptian Longspearmen, Machimoi Phalangites, Machimoi Cavalry and Machairophoroi Cavalry.
  • When Thebes-Megale Diospolis goes over, the Egyptians receive 10,000 denarii.
  • At the same time an army led by the general Ahmose appears near Thebes-Megale Diospolis: Machimoi Cavalry, two Machimoi Phalangites, two Egyptian Longspearmen, two Machimoi Swordsmen, two Egyptian Skirmishers, two Machimoi Archers and two Egyptian Slingers. It is placed on the first of 3 set spots that no character is standing on; if all 3 are occupied, no army appears.
  • The Egyptians are always at war with the Ptolemaic Empire: if they are ever at peace, they declare war again at the start of their turn.
  • The revolt happens only once per campaign.

Playing as the rebels

If a human is playing the Ptolemaic Empire when the revolt breaks out, a pop-up titled 'The Great Egyptian Revolt' asks whether they want to leave their campaign and play the Egyptians. Accepting switches the player to the Egyptians and adds a government building in Syene-Elephantine, Thebes-Megale Diospolis, Mikra Apollonos Polis, Megale Apollonos Polis and Panos Polis. The Egyptians can also be chosen at the start of a new campaign, in which case the revolt happens straight away.

The in-game message: The Great Egyptian Revolt

Following Ptolemy IV’s recent unexpected victory at the Battle of Raphia, aided by his native Egyptian subjects, the latter’s pride knows no boundaries. War has erupted all over Egypt, the Nile running red with the blood of its victims. Now, only the gods know who the next pharaoh will be…

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

The native Egyptians regularly rose up in rebellion against their Ptolemaic overlords. The most significant rebellion was the so called Great Revolt (205-186 BC), during which the Ptolemaic kings lost large parts of Upper Egypt in a gruelling guerilla war. Under the reign of pharaoh Hyrgonaphor (Haronnophris) and, later on, his successor Chaonnophris, the Egyptians aimed at establishing a new native dynasty that would end foreign rule, which had largely prevailed since the arrival of the Persians under Kambyses in the late 6th century BC. In the event, the Egyptians would eventually be overcome, but in RIS it is in your hands to change the course of history.
