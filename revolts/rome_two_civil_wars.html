The Roman Civil Wars

Breaks away: Roman Rebels (roman_rebels_1), Rome, Roman Rebels (roman_rebels_2) and The Roman Senate from Rome in the first war; in the second, whichever of Rome or the first Roman Rebels faction won the first war

When a human plays Rome and the Republic grows very large, it can tear itself apart in two civil wars. In the first, from turn 122 (240 BC) at the earliest, Rome loses its settlements in five legion recruiting areas to the Roman Rebels (the Optimates), and you may choose which side to lead. Once one side has crushed the other, a second, four-way war can follow, in which the winner splits into the loser of the first war, a second Roman Rebels faction and The Roman Senate.

Only in a campaign where a human plays Rome; with a computer-run Rome the first civil war never breaks out, so neither war happens.

What sets it off

  • First civil war: From turn 122 (240 BC), at the start of Rome's turn, Rome must hold more than 150 settlements, and the Italic revolt must already have happened and be over.
  • First civil war: Rome must also hold 5 or more regions in each of at least 5 legion recruiting areas; the city of Rome's own region counts as an area on its own.
  • First civil war: The split then comes at the end of a Roman turn on which Rome has a family general standing outside a settlement and holds 5 or more regions in at least one of the five disloyal areas: Germanica, Gallica, Cyrenaica, Parthica or Alaudae.
  • Second civil war: It can only follow once the first war is decided, meaning the side you play has cut the other side down to a single settlement, and 2 turns have passed with the loser still on that one settlement.
  • Second civil war: The winner of the first war must hold more than 200 settlements, the loser must still hold exactly one, and the winner must hold 5 or more regions in at least 6 of the 10 legion recruiting areas (the city of Rome's region does not count here).
  • Second civil war: While all of that is true, there is a 35% chance at the start of each of the winner's turns that the war is set in motion. The provinces rise at the end of the winner's third turn, counting the turn it was set in motion.

What happens

  • First civil war: A pop-up, 'A Divided Republic', asks whether you want to switch to the Optimates (the first Roman Rebels faction) or remain with the Populares (Rome). Whatever you choose, the revolt goes ahead.
  • First civil war: Every settlement Rome holds in the Germanica, Gallica, Cyrenaica, Parthica or Alaudae areas, wherever Rome has 5 or more regions in that area, rebels and joins the Roman Rebels at the start of the next turn. The Ferrata, Paterna, Hispana, Equestris and Gemina areas and the city of Rome stay loyal.
  • First civil war: The breakaway settlements are held by garrisons of Leves Armaturae and Legionary Cohort units with a strength multiplier of 3.5.
  • First civil war: Rome and the Roman Rebels are at war. Every turn, all the Roman factions on the map (Rome, both Roman Rebels factions and The Roman Senate) are kept at war with each other.
  • First civil war, if you stay with Rome: all Roman family members except the faction leader are marked as proscribed, and the Roman Rebels leader stays on their small island in the corner of the map, unable to move or die.
  • First civil war, if you switch sides: the Roman Rebels receive 30,000, one of Rome's generals out in the field is sent to the far corner of the map by the island, where he cannot move or die, and the rebel characters other than their leader are marked as proscribed. On the next turn the Roman Rebels leader is moved from the island to a free landing spot in central Italy with extra movement points, the island passes to Rome, and you take control of the Roman Rebels.
  • First civil war: The war counts as decided when your side has cut the other side down to one settlement, normally the island.
  • Second civil war: The winner's largest legion recruiting area where it holds 5 or more regions breaks away to the loser of the first war: to the first Roman Rebels faction if you won as Rome, or to Rome if you won as the rebels.
  • Second civil war: The next largest such area breaks away to the second Roman Rebels faction.
  • Second civil war: The city of Rome's region breaks away to The Roman Senate, which appears for the first time. All four Roman factions are at war with each other.
  • Both civil wars: While you play any Roman faction and have taken at least 2 settlements from another Roman faction you are at war with, and it is down to fewer than 6 settlements, there is a 20% chance each turn that it offers to surrender (for The Roman Senate, fewer than 12 but more than 6). Accepting hands you all its settlements except its island and destroys its armies; refusing lets the war go on. Rome and the first Roman Rebels can each offer this once in each war; the second Roman Rebels and the Senate once.
  • Second civil war: Once the offers to switch sides have been answered, any computer-run Roman faction left with only its island settlement loses it to the Free Peoples and drops out.

Playing as the rebels

Yes, if you play Rome. In the first war the 'A Divided Republic' pop-up lets you switch to the Optimates (the first Roman Rebels faction); you take control of them on the next turn, once their leader has landed in central Italy, and they receive 30,000. In the second war, once all three breakaway factions are on the map, you get a chain of offers, each made only once: first to play the loser of the first war ('Defenders of the Old Order' for the first Roman Rebels if you are Rome, or 'Champions of the People' for Rome if you are the rebels), then 'A New Power Arises' for the second Roman Rebels faction, then 'The Will of the Senate' for The Roman Senate. Declining one moves you on to the next, and accepting ends the chain. If you pick Rome or a Roman Rebels faction, its leader is first brought to a landing spot in central Italy with extra movement, its island is handed to the Free Peoples, and you take control on the next turn; switching to the Senate happens at once. If you decline every offer, you keep playing your current faction.

Major disagreements between the supporters of the Senate/Optimates and the supporters of the people/Populares have escalated into open war and now Roman generals march with their own legions into Rome. Some say that the Populares demand radical land reforms and cater to the pleb, threatening to change the traditions and way of life by imposing bills that will affect the wealth of the elite in favour of the masses. The Optimates, the "Best Men" of Rome, fight to keep our traditions, order, and the authority of the Senate. Will you favour the populist cause and fight for the people, or side with the traditionalist, and march against their enemies to restore order in the Roman Republic?
