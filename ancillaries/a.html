Aeneas - The Tactician’s

Effects: +1 Siege Defence

Aineas Taktikos was a general who lived in the Classical times, although nobody is certain as to the exact dates of his birth and death, who wrote a number of treatises on the subject of warfare. King Pyrrhos is reputed to have been one of his admirers. “Poliorketike” is a monograph outlining measures a commander is to take when under siege. Since Alexander, siege craft has evolved greatly, so one will not find in Aineas’ book the most useful counter-measures against wall-shattering ballistas or tunneling, but the wise general will heed his advice on how to deal with faction and treachery, as dangerous now as it had been in his times.

Acquisition is tied to the trait: Interested in Military Arts.

Aenesidemus

unique — one in the world at a time · never alongside Judge, Honest Man.

Effects: +2 Management, +2 Unrest, -2 Law

A Greek skeptical philosopher, he taught in Alexandria and flourished shortly after the life of Cicero. His school is most commonly referred to as Pyrrhonism, but also as the third skeptic school. His chief work, the Pyrrhoneia discussed four main ideas: the reasons for scepticism and doubt, arguments against causality and truth, a physical theory and an ethical theory.
