Liberation

A choice has been made to grant the citizens of this settlement full autonomy!

To rule everything is exhausting; sometimes, it is easier to let others do it for you, under close supervision, of course

Rome’s power is not always wielded through the sword. There are times when wisdom dictates that granting a province a semblance of autonomy is far more beneficial than governing it directly. By allowing local elites to remain in control, the Republic avoids unnecessary uprisings, maintains stability, and, most importantly, ensures that taxes and tribute continue to flow, voluntarily, of course. But make no mistake: Rome does not simply let go. Even when a province is granted self-rule, the shadow of the Republic looms over it. Freed cities quickly find that their armies still march with Roman steel, their trade routes still follow Roman roads, and their leaders remain ever conscious of Rome’s favour, or Rome’s displeasure. True autonomy? Perhaps. True independence? Never.

Strategy

By constructing this building, you will give this city freedom and retreat your forces from it in the next turn! Be aware that if this city is part of the homeland of another faction, it will most certainly revolt back to that faction.

Wording varies by culture: 7 versions of this description exist in the game files.
